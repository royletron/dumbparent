## Before you start

YouTube Kids is a free app from Google that shows videos curated for children.
It is **separate** from the main YouTube app and has its own content filters and
parental controls. You do **not** need a Google account for your child to use it —
but you will need one to manage settings from your own device.

<Callout type="info" title="What you'll need">
- The child's iPad running iPadOS 16 or later (check in **Settings → General → About**)
- A Google account for yourself (a Gmail address is fine)
- About 15 minutes, plus a few minutes to review settings together
</Callout>

---

## Step 1 — Download YouTube Kids

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the App Store on the iPad">
    Tap the blue **App Store** icon on the iPad's home screen.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Search for 'YouTube Kids'">
    Tap **Search** at the bottom, then type **YouTube Kids** in the search bar
    and press **Search** on the keyboard.

    Download the app made by **Google LLC** — it has a red icon with a white
    play button and a small child figure. Do not confuse it with the regular
    YouTube app.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Install and open the app">
    Tap **Get**, then **Install**. You may need to enter your Apple ID password
    or use Face ID / Touch ID. Once installed, tap **Open**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 2 — Set up a parent account

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap 'Sign in as a parent'">
    On the welcome screen, tap **Sign in as a parent** (not "I'm a kid"). You
    will be asked to sign in with your Google account.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verify you are a parent">
    Google will ask you to confirm you are an adult by entering your date of birth
    and agreeing to the terms. Follow the on-screen steps.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your child's profile">
    Tap **Add profile**, enter your child's first name and their date of birth.
    YouTube Kids uses the birthday to set an initial content level — you can
    change this in the next section.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 3 — Choose the right content level

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the content settings for your child's profile">
    After creating the profile, tap the **padlock icon** (bottom of the screen),
    enter your Google password to unlock parent settings, then tap your child's
    name and choose **Settings**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the content level">
    Tap **Content level** and choose the option that fits your child:

    | Level | Best for | What's included |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Preschool** | Under 5 | Very simple, bright videos: nursery rhymes, basic learning |
    | **Younger** | 5–7 | Cartoons, simple how-to videos, music |
    | **Older** | 8–12 | More variety, gaming, science, longer content |

    <Callout type="tip">
      When in doubt, start one level lower than you think. You can always move
      up, and it's easier to loosen restrictions than explain why something
      slipped through.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn off Search (recommended for under-8s)" optional>
    Tap **Search** and toggle it **off**. This prevents your child from
    searching for anything and limits them to curated content only.

    <Callout type="warning">
      Search is one of the main ways children find content outside their age
      level. We recommend disabling it for children under 8.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on 'Only approved content'" optional>
    Toggle on **Only show content I've approved**. With this on, your child
    can only watch videos, channels, and collections that you have specifically
    approved from your own YouTube Kids app.

    This is the most restrictive — and most secure — option, but it does mean
    you'll need to keep adding content they enjoy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 4 — Lock the app with a timer and passcode

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set a timer (Screen Time)">
    Back in **parent settings**, tap **Timer** to set a daily time limit. Once
    the timer runs out, the app locks until you enter your password.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Block the regular YouTube app using Screen Time on the iPad">
    Go to **Settings → Screen Time** on the iPad. If Screen Time isn't turned on,
    tap **Turn On Screen Time** and set a passcode your child doesn't know.

    Then go to **Content & Privacy Restrictions → Allowed Apps** and make sure the
    regular **YouTube** app is **not installed** — delete it if it is.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What your child can access

<WhatToExpect>
  <CanDo>Watch videos from channels curated by YouTube for their age group</CanDo>
  <CanDo>Search for topics (if you leave Search enabled)</CanDo>
  <CanDo>Like videos and add them to a Favourites collection</CanDo>
  <CannotDo>Access regular YouTube or log in to a YouTube/Google account</CannotDo>
  <CannotDo>See comments on videos (comments are hidden in YouTube Kids)</CannotDo>
  <CannotDo>Post comments, upload videos, or interact with other users</CannotDo>
  <CannotDo>Disable safe search or change content filters themselves</CannotDo>
</WhatToExpect>

## Side doors to watch out for

YouTube Kids is good, but it is not perfect. Here is what can still slip through:

<Callout type="danger" title="Side door: Search results">
  Even with content level filtering on, Search can surface videos that have
  slipped through YouTube's automated filters. A child searching for a
  cartoon character can sometimes find fan-made or parody content that is
  not appropriate.

  **What to do:** Disable Search for younger children. For older children,
  check **Watch History** (parent settings → History) weekly.
</Callout>

<Callout type="danger" title="Side door: Autoplay and recommended videos">
  After a video ends, YouTube Kids automatically plays a suggested video.
  Occasionally a video that slipped through moderation can appear here.

  **What to do:** Use the Timer feature to limit total watch time. Sit with
  your child occasionally and watch what autoplay serves up.
</Callout>

<Callout type="danger" title="Side door: Browser-based YouTube">
  If Safari or Chrome is available on the iPad, a determined child can
  navigate to youtube.com directly.

  **What to do:** In **Screen Time → Content & Privacy → Content Restrictions →
  Web Content**, choose **Allowed Websites Only** or **Limit Adult Websites**
  and block youtube.com.
</Callout>

## Tips for ongoing management

- **Check watch history weekly** — go to parent settings → History to see
  exactly what your child has watched.
- **Use the parent app on your phone** — the YouTube Kids app on your own
  device lets you approve or block individual channels, set timers, and get
  an overview of viewing habits.
- **Have the chat** — explain to your child that you can see what they watch,
  not to spy on them, but because you care. Children who know parents are
  gently involved are less likely to seek out problematic content.
- **Report bad content** — if you see something that shouldn't be there, tap
  the three dots on the video and choose **Report**. Google reviews these
  and it helps improve filtering for all families.
