## Before you start

Amazon Fire tablets are one of the most popular choices for younger children
because they come with **Amazon Kids** built in — a fully managed environment
that wraps the entire tablet in child-friendly controls. You can subscribe to
**Amazon Kids+** (a content library) separately, but the parental controls
themselves are free.

<Callout type="info" title="What you'll need">
- The Amazon Fire tablet (any generation from 2019 onwards)
- Your Amazon account credentials (the account the tablet is registered to)
- About 20 minutes, plus the tablet charged to at least 50%
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  The steps below are for the standard Fire OS interface. If the tablet already
  has Amazon Kids turned on (common with the Kids Edition tablets), skip to
  **Step 3** to review the settings.
</Callout>

---

## Step 1 — Update the software

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check for updates">
    Swipe down from the top of the screen to open **Quick Settings**, then tap
    **Settings** (the cog icon) → **Device Options** → **System Updates** →
    **Check Now**.

    Install any available updates. The tablet may restart.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 2 — Set up Amazon Kids

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings and find Amazon Kids">
    Tap **Settings** → **Parental Controls**. You may be asked to create a
    parental controls PIN — choose something your child won't guess (not their
    birthday). Write it down and keep it somewhere safe.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on Amazon Kids">
    Scroll down to **Amazon Kids** and tap **Turn On Amazon Kids**. Follow the
    prompts to create a child profile.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your child's name and age">
    Enter your child's first name and date of birth. Amazon uses the birthday
    to tailor the content library. You can create profiles for multiple
    children and switch between them.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose an age group for content">
    Select the age range that best matches your child. You can fine-tune this
    later in the Parent Dashboard.

    | Age range | What's typically included |
    |---|---|
    | 3–5 years | Simple games, colouring, nursery rhyme videos |
    | 6–8 years | Books, educational games, age-appropriate videos |
    | 9–12 years | More advanced books, games, selected web access |
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 3 — Configure time limits

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set a daily screen time limit">
    In **Amazon Kids settings** → **Daily Goals & Time Limits**, you can set:
    - A **total screen time** limit per day
    - Separate limits for **videos**, **apps**, and **books**
    - A **bedtime** after which the tablet won't work
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set a bedtime">
    Tap **Curfew** and set the times when the tablet should be unavailable. For
    example, 8:00 pm to 7:00 am means the tablet won't work overnight.

    <Callout type="tip">
      It's worth setting a bedtime even if you also have a time limit — the
      bedtime acts as a hard stop regardless of how much screen time has been
      used.
    </Callout>
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 4 — Review content settings

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check what apps and content are available">
    From **Amazon Kids settings** → **Content**, you can see everything that
    has been added to your child's library. Remove anything that doesn't look
    right for their age.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Disable the web browser (recommended for under-10s)" optional>
    Tap **Web Browser** and toggle it **off**. The Fire's Silk browser, if
    left on, can be used to access websites outside of Amazon's filters.

    <Callout type="warning">
      For children under 10, we strongly recommend disabling web browsing
      entirely. For older children, Amazon Kids includes a filtered web
      browser — review the allowed categories carefully.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn off Amazon Shopping">
    In **Amazon Kids settings**, make sure **In-App Purchasing** is set to
    **Blocked** or requires your PIN. This prevents accidental (or deliberate)
    purchases.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Step 5 — Set up the Parent Dashboard

<Steps>
  <Step title="Visit the Amazon Parent Dashboard">
    On your own device or computer, go to **parents.amazon.co.uk** and sign in
    with the same Amazon account as the tablet.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Explore what you can manage remotely">
    From the dashboard you can:
    - See daily screen time and activity summaries
    - Pause the tablet instantly
    - Add or remove content
    - Adjust time limits without touching the tablet
  </Step>
</Steps>

<WhatToExpect>
  <CanDo>Apps, games, books, and videos you've approved in the Amazon Kids library</CanDo>
  <CanDo>Age-filtered web browsing (if you leave the browser enabled)</CanDo>
  <CanDo>Amazon Kids+ content library (if you subscribe — separate monthly cost)</CanDo>
  <CannotDo>Exit Amazon Kids without your PIN</CannotDo>
  <CannotDo>Download new apps without your approval</CannotDo>
  <CannotDo>Make purchases</CannotDo>

## Side doors to watch out for

<Callout type="danger" title="Side door: Knowing your PIN">
  If a child watches you enter the parental PIN or figures it out, they can
  exit Amazon Kids and use the tablet without restrictions.

  **What to do:** Choose a PIN that is not your birthday or a number your child
  could guess. Change it occasionally. Never enter it while your child is watching.
</Callout>

<Callout type="danger" title="Side door: Other Amazon accounts">
  If the tablet has other Amazon accounts registered, a child might be able to
  switch accounts and access unfiltered content.

  **What to do:** In **Settings → Account & Lists**, ensure only your account
  is registered on the device.
</Callout>

<Callout type="danger" title="Side door: Connecting to a friend's WiFi">
  Amazon Kids controls are device-level and work regardless of which WiFi the
  tablet is on. However, if the browser is enabled, connecting to a different
  network doesn't bypass filters — they are applied by Amazon's servers.
  This is relatively low risk.
</Callout>

## Tips for ongoing management

- **Check the Parent Dashboard weekly** — it shows you at a glance what your
  child has been doing and flags anything unusual.
- **Adjust content as they grow** — children grow fast. Revisit the age settings
  every six months or so to make sure the content level still fits.
- **Add books to their library** — Amazon Kids gives you access to thousands of
  books. Adding books increases the chance they pick reading over video time.
- **Talk about it** — let your child know the tablet has these limits, and why.
  Children who understand the "why" are more likely to respect the boundaries.
